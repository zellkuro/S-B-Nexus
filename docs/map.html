The Black Thorn Blight

Brightwater used to be a minor shipping town. A few years ago the cargo ship Gull's Mercy wrecked on Tideglass Isle carrying blackberry cuttings bound for a farm further down the coast, and the cuttings took root. Nobody knew blackberries would grow so well here. At first it was a blessing: the herbalist found the berries make health potions, and Brightwater became a potion town. Then the brambles kept growing. Now black thorn is pushing up through the woods all along the coast: briars that grow overnight, berries gone glossy black with a violet glow, and animals that eat them turning savage. Brightwater can't clear it alone, so the town council grants sellswords a Thorn Writ: a contract giving them limited authority to act on the town's behalf. That's you, and everyone else in the woods.

The berries

Ripe blackberries still grow on healthy hedgerows. Eating one (raise it to your mouth) heals almost nothing, but the herbalist brews them into health potions: that's where every potion on the coast comes from. Blight berries are glossy black with a violet glow. Never eat them: collect them for the herbalist, who burns them. Wolves and goblins eat them, and that's what turns them.

The Thorn Writ

The council doesn't recruit you into anything: it grants you a limited contract. Your writ lets you bear arms in the woods, burn thorn on other people's land and claim bounties, but only in the lands it names. Prove yourself and the council extends it: the Glade first, then the Hollow, the Ruins, and finally the Isle. Bounties under the writ are posted on the board in the square.

Everyone in the same woods

Every finished contract pushes a zone's thorn level down. When a zone's thorn gets too high, a Thorn Surge contract appears: a thorn brute and a wave of creatures, open to anyone in the zone, and everyone who hits it gets paid. Once an hour, a contract to burn back the Thornheart opens on the island. No party needed: just turn up.

    Why it fits an MMO: nobody is the one hero, the thorn grows back (which explains respawning enemies and the hourly Thornheart), and every sellsword holds the same kind of writ, so solo players end up in the same places at the same time without joining anything. One world or several? Each zone could be its own small Altspace world, joined to the city by a portal (violet rings), with progress carried by the backend.
